Newton's Law of Cooling says a hot object left in a room doesn't cool down at a constant rate — it cools quickly at first, then more and more slowly as its temperature approaches the room's. The object's temperature at time t (in minutes) is given by:

T(t) = T_room + (T0 - T_room) * e^(-t / tau)

where T0 is the object's starting temperature, T_room is the (constant) room temperature, and tau is the time constant, in minutes — how quickly the object cools. This is the same kind of exponential behavior as an RC circuit's charging and discharging curves, applied to temperature instead of voltage.
